WetSimFX

Blood, mud and water that behave like the real thing, simulated on your GPU in real time inside Blender. Objects push, smear and splash the fluid, carry it away on their surfaces, and leave tracks that dry.

Fresh Blood preset: a plank dragged through a pool. Rendered in EEVEE from a baked simulation.

  • Blender 5.3 or newer
  • Renderers EEVEE and Cycles
  • Presets Fresh Blood, Old Blood, Mud, Water
  • Output EXR sequence, no add-on needed at render time
  • Solver 2D height field (not a 3D fluid sim)

Install

  1. Download wetsimfx-<version>.zip. Do not unzip it.
  2. In Blender open Edit › Preferences › Get Extensions, click the ⌄ menu at the top right and pick Install from Disk, then choose the zip.
  3. Open the 3D Viewport sidebar (N). The WetSimFX tab is there.

Upgrading from the old Viscous Fluid RT? Uninstall it first. Scenes made with it keep their settings: WetSimFX reads them.

A graphics card with current drivers is needed: the simulation runs on the GPU. Any card that runs Blender 5.3's EEVEE well is enough; more video memory allows the 2048 resolution.

Quick start

Five minutes from an empty scene to a rendered smear.

  1. Pick a preset by clicking the big thumbnail at the top of the panel.
  2. Set the Surface. Click Create Surface for a new fluid plane, or pick your own floor or ground object in the Surface field: a fluid surface is fitted on top of it and your object is left as it is.
  3. Add the objects. Put the objects that should push the fluid (a foot, a tyre, a plank) in a collection and pick it as Brushes. Put a few flat shapes where the pools or puddles should start in another collection and pick it as Sources. Sources never render.
  4. Press Live and move or play your animation: the fluid reacts in the viewport. Reset puts it back to the start.
  5. Bake Frames when the motion is right. The material switches to the baked frames, so you can render with EEVEE, Cycles, from the command line or on a render farm.

Presets

Each preset is a full set of physical numbers and its own look. Switching preset on a surface made by WetSimFX rebuilds its material at once. The box under Live shows only the few settings that change that preset's look most.

Brushes & Sources

Brushes

Any mesh in the Brushes collection displaces the fluid where its underside reaches it: a sliding object plows a bow wave and leaves a thin streaky film, a rolling one presses ruts with berms on both sides, a falling one splashes. Animated, simulated or hand-moved objects all work. Faster objects are handled with more substeps automatically; in mud, an object dragged very fast sheds the top of its bow pile as flying clods instead of stacking it up.

Objects that touched the fluid get coated where they touched it once you press Dirty the Brushes (or Wet the Brushes for water): a ball rolled through blood carries a band round its middle, a board dragged through mud sinks into the channel it cuts.

Sources

Draw the outline of a pool or puddle with a flat mesh and put it in the Sources collection. At Reset its footprint fills to Pool Depth. Two optional custom properties on a source object (Object Properties › Custom Properties):

PropertyMeaningExample
vf_depthThis source's own pool depth, in metres, instead of Pool Depth.0.006 = 6 mm
vf_rateKeeps pouring while the sim runs, in metres of thickness per second.0.002 = a steady trickle

An object can be a source and a brush at the same time, for example a leaking bag that is dragged across the floor.

Live, Bake, Render

Live is for finding the motion: it runs as fast as your card allows and reacts while you move things. It is not frame-exact. Bake Frames steps through the frame range at a fixed rate and writes two EXR sequences per frame (<Surface>_0001.exr and, when the look needs it, <Surface>_b_0001.exr). The material reads them like any image sequence, so renders need no Python and no add-on: send the .blend and the bake folder to a farm.

For EEVEE, turn on Raytracing in the render settings: wet reflections and puddles depend on it. Cycles needs nothing extra.

Tips & limits

A 2D solver, not a 3D one

WetSimFX simulates the fluid as a height field: for every spot on the surface it tracks how thick the layer is and how it flows sideways, the way a thin film of blood, mud or water really behaves on the ground. This is what makes it real-time and lets it model what thin layers do: weight, spreading, smearing, ruts and drying.

It is not a 3D volume simulation like Mantaflow or FLIP. What that means in practice:

  • One fluid layer per spot: no fluid flowing under itself, no curling or breaking waves, no tunnels.
  • No falling streams or drops in the air. Splash droplets land as splats where they would fall, without a flight in between.
  • The fluid stays on the surface. A bucket being poured, a jet or a deep tank needs a 3D fluid sim; WetSimFX handles the puddle, the smear and the tracks they leave.

Tips

  • Scale matters. The fluid uses real units: a 6 m surface at 1024 has 6 mm cells. Keep objects at real size.
  • Close-ups: shrink Size to the area you need before raising Resolution. A 2 m area at 1024 is sharper than 6 m at 2048 and four times faster.
  • Floors and gentle ground only in this version: the fluid lies on a flat or gently uneven surface. Walls, stairs and other objects come in version 2.6.
  • Water in EEVEE is drawn inside your floor's own material (a wet layer and a clear coat), because EEVEE cannot reflect through a see-through surface. If your floor does not use a Principled BSDF, an approximate look is used.
  • Live frame rate drops with fast objects and 2048. Use 512 or 1024 while animating; bake at the final setting.

Troubleshooting

I press Live and nothing happens.

Check that a Surface is set and that the Sources collection has objects (or Fill Depth is above 0). Brushes only move fluid that is there. Sources must overlap the fluid area.

The fluid does not react to my object.

The object must be a mesh in the Brushes collection and its underside must reach the fluid surface. Objects floating above it do nothing.

The render shows the fluid from Live, not the bake.

Press Stop, then Bake Frames. After a bake the material reads the baked frames; Live switches it back.

The baked frames are missing on another computer or the farm.

Keep the Bake Folder relative (//fluid_cache/) and copy that folder along with the .blend.

A very fast object leaves a tall wall of fluid.

Raise Spatter so the pile breaks into splash, or raise Max Substeps in Advanced.

Live is slow.

Lower Resolution to 512 while animating, or reduce Size. The first start can take a few seconds while Blender compiles the GPU shaders.
